written into the text itself. The reader draws them on the page; a reader
that does not know them shows their text instead. There are no image
files: everything below is part of this article's few kilobytes.

# How it is written

A picture is a block that starts with a line of three backquotes, the
word path and its width and height in screen units, and ends with a line
of three backquotes. In between is path data as in SVG: M moves, L, H and
V draw lines, C, S, Q and T draw curves, Z closes a shape, and lowercase
letters take coordinates relative to the last point. The words fill and
stroke, each with a colour such as #61afef (and for stroke a width), set
how the shapes after them are drawn; fg is the colour of the text. This
one is shown as text because it is indented by four spaces:

    ```path 120 60
    fill #e06c75 stroke fg 2 M10 50 L60 10 110 50 Z
    ```

A picture wider than the page is scaled down to fit. Arcs (A) are not
supported yet: a picture with one is shown as text.
