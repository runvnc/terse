Welcome to Terse. This is the reader, a small program running inside the
Terse machine: a 32-bit RISC-V computer with a fixed budget of work per
frame, a text terminal, a page of typeset text and a content device that
moves articles between hosts. Everything you see here was drawn by the
program; the host only lays the text out with its own fonts.

Three more articles came with it. Press Tab to move to a link and Enter
to follow it (or click it); Esc brings you back here:

- [How Terse works](terse:LONG), a longer read
- [Hello in three languages](terse:LANGUAGES)
- [Pictures in articles](terse:PICTURES)

# Writing an article

An article is plain text. Paragraphs are separated by blank lines, and the
line breaks inside a paragraph do not matter: the reader joins the lines
and the host wraps them to fit. A line that starts with # and a space is a
heading, lines that start with - and a space are a list, and lines
indented by four spaces are kept exactly as written, in a monospaced face,
which is handy for tables, code and the occasional bit of ASCII art:

    Key          What it does
    Up, Down     move through the timeline
    Enter        open the selected article
    Tab          the next link in an article
    /            search for feeds and articles
    F            manage the feeds you follow
    ?            help, and your own feed key
    Esc          back, or quit from the timeline

Lines that start with | make a real table, laid out by the host; a
line of dashes under the first row makes it a header:

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| M | open the Terse manual, which says all of this in detail |
| T | publish a test article of your own |

A word between backquotes, like `this`, is shown as code.

A link is written as [text](address), as in the list at the top, and its
text is shown in a link colour. A link to another article is
terse: followed by its ID, the 64 hex digits of its hash; one to a feed
is terse:feed/ followed by the feed's key; one to a program is
terse:program/, the publisher's key, / and the program's name. Written
out on their own, without brackets, they are links too. Links lead only
to articles, feeds and programs on the Terse network: a web address is
ordinary text.

A block between two lines of three backquotes is kept as written too;
one that starts with ```path holds a small drawing (see "Pictures in
articles").

# Feeds

Each article belongs to a feed, a numbered series of entries signed with
one person's key. Your host made a key for you when this demo started, and
these articles are the first entries of your own feed. Press F to see
the feeds you follow, and ? to see your key in full: give it to a friend
and they can subscribe to whatever you publish.

Your feed is named "demo" for now. In the feeds view, P sets the name
others see beside your key (and whether search may find it), and N gives
any feed a nickname that only you see. Names are not unique, so the
reader always shows the key beside a name. Press / to search your relays
for feeds by name and for articles by title.

To read someone else's feed, press F, then S, and type or paste their key.
The host asks you before any program subscribes, unsubscribes or publishes
in your name; that prompt is drawn by the host, not by the program, and the
program cannot answer it for you.
