Write and publish an article

An article is a plain text file. Write it in any text editor and save it as hello.txt:

# Hello, Tersenet

This is my first article. Lines next to each other
are joined into one paragraph.

## What I like

- short pages
- no adverts
- `code` in backquotes

| Day | Walked |
|---|--:|
| Monday | 4 km |
| Tuesday | 6 km |

A blank line ends a paragraph. # starts a heading and ## a smaller one; - starts an item of a list; text between backquotes is code; lines that start with | are a table, and the |---|--:| line under its first row makes that row a header and puts the second column on the right.

Is it Markdown?

It looks like Markdown, and what the two have in common is written the same way as in GitHub-flavoured Markdown: paragraphs, # and ## headings, lists with - or *, code blocks (indented four spaces, or between lines of three backquotes), code spans, tables, and links written [text](target). But it is not strictly a subset of it. It is much smaller, and a few things differ:

  • Not in Tersenet, and shown just as you typed them: emphasis and bold (*, _), headings smaller than ##, numbered and nested lists, quotes (>), images, HTML, rules (---), backslash escapes, and links to web pages: a link's target must be a terse: address.
  • Tersenet's own: terse: links to articles, feeds and programs, and figures: drawings, written as a fence whose opening backquotes are followed by path and a size.
  • Read differently: a table needs no |---| line (without one it simply has no header), and a line indented four spaces starts a code block even right after a paragraph's line.

So an article that keeps to what the two share shows much the same on GitHub, and a Markdown file mostly reads well in Tersenet, with its emphasis marks showing. Writing an article shows every rule.

Publish it

terse feed publish hello.txt --title "Hello, Tersenet"
seq 2 blob 5d86159d495906bb…ad537da380d

The article is now entry #2 of your feed (your profile was #1), signed with your key, on your relay. Anyone who can reach that relay can read it. The title is what timelines show; the heading at the top of the text is for people who open the article by a link.

See it

The timeline shows the feeds you follow, so follow your own:

terse feed subscribe $(terse key show)
subscribed to 7105227f05be8a2efd…6854b14db7680ef9987e084c

(That works in bash, zsh, fish and PowerShell; or paste your key after subscribe.) The window reads the list of feeds you follow when it starts, so close it, run terse-host again and start the reader:

Timeline
1 article  ·  1 feed
Hello, Tersenet
Sam (710522…7e084c)  ·  2026-11-22 00:17  ·  233 bytes

Enter opens it: the title, a grey line with your name, the entry number, the date and the size, then the text, with the paragraph wrapped to the column, "What I like" in bold, the list with bullets, code in a monospaced face and the table as a grid with its header in bold. Esc goes back to the timeline.

The heading is the title, so the reader shows it once. A shortcut for trying this: T in the reader publishes a short test article and follows your feed for you, after the window asks you.

What you have now

A feed with an article in it, and a reader that shows it. Next, the reader itself: Read articles and follow links.
