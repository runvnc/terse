Terse Manual

Writing a reader without the machine

The core of Terse is the network and the article format, not the machine. A reader that implements just those two, natively, in any language and with any text toolkit, is a complete Terse client: it follows feeds, shows their articles and follows their links, side by side with machine hosts on the same relays. This article says what such a reader has to do and where the exact rules are. You need two documents, NETWORK.md and ARTICLE.md; SPEC.md and STATE.md (the machine) are not needed at all.

What it must do

The short list is in NETWORK.md section 6:

  • keep blobs by their BLAKE3-256 ID and verify every blob it receives;
  • talk to at least one relay;
  • verify entries and drop invalid ones;
  • subscribe to feeds by key or feed address (a key, @ and up to two relays that hold the feed: NETWORK.md 4.9) and keep the timeline in arrival order;
  • show articles by ARTICLE.md;
  • show every feed's name with its key.

Everything else is optional: publishing, profiles, nicknames, search, several relays, prefetch.

Blobs and the relay

A relay speaks binary WebSocket messages with little-endian integers. A reader needs four of them, and must ignore the replies it does not use:

MessageLayout
Get01h, req (4), blob ID (32)
Get reply81h, req, status (1), the blob if status is 1
Subscribe04h, req, feed key (32), after (4)
Subscribe reply84h, req, status, highest (4) if status is 1
Unsubscribe05h, req, feed key (32)
Entry (pushed)90h, the entry

req is any number you choose; the reply echoes it, and replies may come in any order. A get reply carries the whole blob: hash it, compare with the ID you asked for, and drop it (as "not found on that relay") if they differ. Keep what you fetched in a local store, a directory with one file per ID is enough, so that an article opens at once the next time.

Entries and the timeline

After a subscribe, the relay sends the feed's entries with seq above after, oldest first, then each new one as it is published. An entry is at most 293 bytes: the feed key, seq, a time, a kind, the blob's size and ID, a reply-to ID, a title, and an Ed25519 signature over terse-entry-0 and the bytes before it. Verify it strictly and check the rest of the rules of NETWORK.md section 4.2; keep the first valid entry of each feed and seq.

Your timeline is the article and document entries (kinds 1 and 4) of your subscribed feeds, newest first in the order you first stored them; keep that order across restarts. Subscribe again on each connection with after set to the highest seq you hold, and fetch each blob of at most 16,384 bytes as its entry arrives, so reading never waits.

Profiles (kind 2) give a feed its display name: the title of the feed's profile entry with the highest seq. Names are not unique, so always show the key, or its first and last six hex digits, beside a name.

Showing an article

ARTICLE.md is written as requirements on what the person sees, not as a layout algorithm, so any toolkit that can wrap text, show a monospaced face and draw curves will do. In outline:

  • Split the text into blocks: headings, subheadings, list items, preformatted blocks, fences, tables and paragraphs, by the first bytes of each line.
  • Join a paragraph's lines and wrap them to your column; keep preformatted lines and fences as written, unwrapped.
  • Find code spans and links on each line, from its start; the one that starts first wins.
  • Lay tables out as grids, or show their lines as a paragraph.
  • Draw figures from their path strings, or show them as their text. A figure's units are 512ths of a column of the recommended width (about 68 characters of body text): scale figures by your column's width over that, down for a narrower column, and up only if you enlarge your text the same way.
  • Anything you do not recognise, or that is malformed, is ordinary text.

Only terse: forms are links: terse: and a blob ID (an article), terse:feed/ and a key, maybe with @ and the hints of its address (a feed; a malformed hint list leaves the key alone), terse:program/, a key and a name (a program), bare or as the target of [text](target). Web addresses are ordinary text.

Programs and documents

Feeds also carry programs (kind 3) and documents (kind 4) for the machine, and may carry kinds a later revision defines (5 to 254), which are valid entries. A reader without the machine keeps or skips such entries without error and, if it shows them, shows them as something it cannot open. A link may also lead to a blob that is a program image (it starts with TERS) or a document (TDOC): say that you cannot open it rather than showing its bytes. A program link gets the same answer.

Checking your reader

Every machine host carries this manual in its store, and the articles use every construct of the format, so they make a good first test: publish them to a feed with terse manual publish, subscribe your reader to it, and compare with the built-in reader. Lines will break differently and fonts will differ; what each construct is, which text is a link and where it leads must not.

The details of the network are in the network; how the built-in reader lays articles out on the machine is in the page device, which you do not need to follow.
