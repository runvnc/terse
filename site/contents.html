Terse Manual

The Terse manual

This manual describes the whole Terse system: the machine, its devices, the network that carries articles and programs, and the programs that come with it. It is written as Terse articles, so you can read it in the reader, follow its links with Tab and Enter, and publish it to a feed. Each article below says what it covers.

Start here

  • What Terse is: what Terse is for, in one page: sharing articles without the web's bloat or a site in control, and, optionally, sharing small programs.
  • How Terse works: the overall picture in two pages: the network and articles, the machine and its frame budget, the devices, determinism and the shell.
  • Writing a program: the toolchain, the C library, building, running a program headless and in the host, and publishing it in a feed.
  • Using the shell and the reader: the keys of the shell and the reader, layouts, sessions and the consent prompts.
  • Writing an article: the article format, every construct shown as source and as it is shown.
  • State, replay and conformance: what a machine's state is, its hash, snapshots and the conformance image.

The machine

  • The machine: the CPU, the memory map, reset, frames and the budget, faults, the image format, the call convention and the whole call table.
  • Events and input: every event, the queue, keys, text, the pointer, the wheel and controllers.
  • Charges and limits: every charge and every numeric limit on one page.

  • The system device: frames, events, time, random bytes, controllers, exit, and asking the shell to open something.
  • The terminal: an 80 by 30 grid of character cells.
  • The 2D vector display: lines and filled shapes from a display list, in a clean or a phosphor style.
  • Content: blobs fetched and published by hash, the feeds the host follows, search, and named records kept on the host.
  • The page: typeset proportional text, figures and tables in a rectangle of the screen.
  • Machines: the shell's device, which starts other machines and lays out their screens, flat or as panels in its 3D scene.
  • The 3D vector display: lines, points and circles in a box behind and in front of the screen, seen from an eye the host chooses.
