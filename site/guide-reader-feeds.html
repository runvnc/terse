Make a reader, step 3: links and feeds

Your reader can show an article and fetch one by its ID. Two things make it a real reader: following links, and following feeds, so that new articles come to you.

Follow a link

A link to an article is terse: and the article's ID. When the person follows one, fetch that ID exactly as in step 2, then show the article. Keep a list of where they came from, so that a key takes them back.

Two other kinds of link exist: terse:feed/ and a feed's key (below), and terse:program/ for a program, which a reader without the virtual machine shows as something it cannot open.

Publish a feed

A feed is one author's numbered series of entries. Each entry is small, at most 293 bytes, and signed with the author's key: it says "number 1 of my feed is this blob, this size, with this title". Make a feed of your own to test with, with the relay from step 2 running:

terse key new
terse feed publish hello.txt --title "Hello"

The first command makes your key, once, and prints it (terse key show prints it again); it is the feed's name. The second puts the article on the relay and publishes entry 1 of your feed.

Subscribe

Send the relay a Subscribe: the byte 04h, a request number (4 bytes), the feed's key (32 bytes) and the highest entry number you already hold (4 bytes, 0 the first time). The relay replies (84h), then sends every entry numbered above that, oldest first, each in a message that starts with 90h, and then each new one as it is published, for as long as you stay connected.

An entry holds, in order: the feed key (32 bytes), its number (4), a time (4), a kind (1: 1 for an article), the blob's size (3) and ID (32), a reply-to ID (32), the title's length (1) and the title, then a 64-byte signature.

Check the signature

Verify each entry's Ed25519 signature before you keep it, and drop the entries that fail. Use a library, but note that an ordinary Ed25519 verify is not quite enough: Tersenet adds a few strict checks, listed in NETWORK.md section 4.2, and vectors/entries.txt holds a forged entry for each, which your reader must refuse.

Then fetch the article each entry names (step 2) and list the titles, newest first: that is your timeline. Show each feed's key, or its first and last six hexadecimal digits, beside anything you show for it, since names are not unique.

With a node (step 2), all of this is two messages: Subscribe (25h, with the feed's key as text) and Timeline (22h), whose entries the node has already checked.

Compare with the reference

tersereader does the same, so you can check your timeline against it:

tersereader relay add ws://127.0.0.1:7878
tersereader subscribe <your feed key>
tersereader sync
tersereader list

What you have now

A Tersenet reader: it follows feeds, checks what it receives, keeps it, and shows articles with their links. NETWORK.md section 6 lists what a complete reader must do; most of it is above. The rest, such as display names, feed addresses with relay hints and the speed requirements, is in Writing a reader without the machine.

Next, if you want to go further, Tersenet can also carry small programs, run by the Terse32 virtual machine. Everything so far works without it. Write a program.
