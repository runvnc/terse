Your feed without the command line

Everything you do with your own side of Tersenet, the window does on screens of its own: make the key that signs what you publish, give yourself a name, publish an article, follow other people's feeds and choose relays. No terminal is needed. The next pages show the same steps as commands too, and say more about each.

Open your screens

With the window open on the launcher, press F10. The window draws its own screens over everything under the bar at the top:

 You ·  Signature   Profile   Publish   Follow   Relays

  You have no signature yet.

  Your signature is a key this computer makes. Everything you publish is
  signed with it, so anyone can check that it came from you. Nobody issues it
  and nobody can take it away: there is no account and no password.

   Create my signature
   Use a key file I already have…

  It will be kept in /home/me/.config/terse/identity.key.

 ←→ or 1-5 choose a page   ↓ Enter go in   Esc or F10 closes

These screens belong to the window, not to a program: no program can see them or what you type into them, and none ever gets your secret key. F10 again, Esc or F12 closes them; the programs go on as they were.

Along the top are five pages. On that top row Left and Right change the page; elsewhere 1 to 5 do, or a click on the page's name. Up, Down and Tab move from one thing to the next, Enter (or Space, or a click) does it, and in a text field you type, and Ctrl+V pastes. Whatever goes out to the network or cannot be undone asks first, and Y goes ahead, N or Esc does nothing. The last lines of the screen say what happened, in plain words.

Your signature

On a first run the screens open on Signature. Down to "Create my signature", Enter, and it is made:

  Your signature (public key):
    874e906aaacf328f7e9a618fa9d946d2404300fdc4919e26e08219dec45fb7dd

  Your feed address, to give to people:
    874e906aaacf328f7e9a618fa9d946d2404300fdc4919e26e08219dec45fb7dd
   Copy the address
  Only people who use the same relay as you will find it: no public encrypted
  (wss://) relay is in your profile or relay list.

  Its secret half is kept in:
    /home/me/.config/terse/identity.key

  Keep a copy of this file somewhere safe, such as a USB stick. Whoever has it
  can publish as you, and if you lose it you can never add to this feed again.
  Tersenet never shows the secret key itself.

  Your signature is made: 874e90…5fb7dd. Keep a copy of its file (below).

The 64 hexadecimal digits are the public half of your key. Your feed's address is what you give to people; "Copy the address" puts it on the clipboard. The secret half is the file named on the screen, and it is never shown. Keep a copy of that file somewhere safe: whoever has it can publish as you, and if you lose it you can never add to your feed again. If you already have a key file (a copy you kept, or another computer's), "Use a key file I already have…" opens a list of your folders to choose it; the window never replaces a key it already has.

Your profile

Page 2, Profile. Type your name (1 to 48 bytes), a few words about yourself if you like, and choose whether search may find your name (Space switches it). "Save and publish" asks, then publishes:

  Name  Sam
  About I write about birds.
  [x] Let search find my name   Space switches it

  Relays your profile names (encrypted ones only):
    none

   Save and publish

  Published now: “Sam”, entry #1 of your feed, listed.

  Your profile is published as entry #1, and search can find your name.
  Taken by 1 of 1 relays: ws://127.0.0.1:7878

Your profile is the first entry of your feed. Names are not unique, so readers always show your key beside it. The relays listed under the fields are the ones your profile names, so that people can find your feed; only encrypted ones (wss://) are ever written there.

Publish an article

Write an article as a plain text file, in any text editor (the page after next, "Write and publish an article", shows how), and save it in your documents folder. Page 3, Publish: "Choose a file…" lists that folder (Enter opens a folder, Backspace goes up), or type the file's path. Enter on the file shows the article as it will read, and what publishing does with each of its links:

  Preview: “Hello, Tersenet”, 108 bytes

  HELLO, TERSENET

  My first article, published from the window.

  What I like

  • short pages
  • no adverts

 P or Enter publish…   ↑↓ PgUp PgDn scroll   Esc back

P asks, Y publishes it, signed with your key:

  “Hello, Tersenet” is published as entry #2 of your feed.
  Taken by 1 of 1 relays: ws://127.0.0.1:7878

Follow a feed

Page 4, Follow. Paste a feed's address (Ctrl+V) and press Enter to follow it, or type a name and press Enter to search for feeds that let search find them; Enter on one that was found follows it:

  Address or name Sam
   Follow or search

  Found (1): Enter follows
   Sam  874e90…5fb7dd

  Feeds you follow (0): Enter or Delete unfollows
    none yet

   Follow my own feed

  1 feed found: Enter on it follows it.

Below are the feeds you follow; Enter or Delete on one stops following it, after asking. To see your own articles in your timeline, follow your own feed: when you do not, the page offers "Follow my own feed".

Relays

Page 5, Relays. A relay carries your feed to others and theirs to you. The page shows the relays this window uses, then your relay list. With an empty list the window uses the relay on this computer, which only this computer can reach. To share one, type it under "Add a relay": a name such as relay.example.org is reached with encryption (wss://), and a plain relay on your own network is written ws://192.168.1.20:7878. "Options" takes the advanced settings of a relay line, such as a pin= for a relay with its own certificate.

  This window uses:
    ws://127.0.0.1:7878

  Your relay list: Enter or Delete takes one out
   wss://relay.example.org

  Added wss://relay.example.org: Tersenet uses it from its next start.
  Your list now replaces the relay on this computer (ws://127.0.0.1:7878): add
  it too to keep using it.

As the last lines say, your list replaces the relay on this computer: add ws://127.0.0.1:7878 too if you still want it.

Enter or Delete on a relay of the list takes it out, after asking. The list is used from the window's next start.

What you have now

Your key, your name, an article in your feed and someone to follow, without a terminal. Next, the same steps as commands, and more about keys, names and addresses: Create your profile.
