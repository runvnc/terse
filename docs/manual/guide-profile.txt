# Create your profile

On Tersenet nobody gives out accounts: there is no sign-up, no e-mail address and no password. You are a key that your computer makes, and your articles are a feed signed with it. Anyone can check that an article in your feed came from you, and nobody without your key can add to it.

The window does all of this on its own screens: F10, then the Signature and Profile pages (the last page, "Your feed without the command line"). This page does the same in a terminal and says more about each part. The commands go in a terminal, with the window still open. (On Windows, open PowerShell in the folder where you unzipped Tersenet and write `.\terse` for `terse`.)

## Your key

```sh
terse key new
```

```text
terse: new identity saved in /home/me/.config/terse/identity.key
7105227f05be8a2efd2ef3e7d503342cebf594666854b14db7680ef9987e084c
```

The 64 hexadecimal digits are the public half of your key: your feed's address, which you give to people. The file holds the secret half. Keep it safe and keep a copy: whoever has the file can publish as you, and if you lose it you cannot add to your feed again. `terse key show` prints your key whenever you need it, and `terse key import` and a file takes a key you kept as yours (it never replaces one you have).

You can skip this step: the window makes a key by itself the first time something publishes, for example when you press T in the reader.

## Your name

```sh
terse profile set --name Sam --listed
```

```text
seq 1 blob c87e03f4b03f250e…8ce0369e33e (listed)
```

(Before that line `terse` says which relay it used, as every command that uses the network does, and which relays took the profile: `terse: taken by 1 of 1 relays: ws://127.0.0.1:7878`. With several relays it waits for each and warns about any that did not take it; it succeeds when one did. Long numbers are shortened with `…` on these pages.) Your profile is the first entry of your feed. It gives you a name, from 1 to 48 bytes, that readers show beside your key. `--listed` lets search find the name; leave it out and only people who already have your key see it. Names are not unique: two people may both be "Sam", which is why readers always show the key too. Run the command again to change the name.

```sh
terse profile show
```

```text
feed:        7105227f05be8a2e…ebf594666854b14db7680ef9987e084c
name:        Sam
profile:     #1 of 2026-11-22 00:17, blob c87e03f4…0369e33e
listed:      yes
```

Without a terminal: the Profile page (F10) has your name, a description and whether search finds you; in the reader, F opens your feeds and P asks for your name and then whether search may find it. Either way the window asks you to confirm.

## Your address

```sh
terse feed address
```

prints the address to give to people (the Signature page shows it too, with a button that copies it): your key, and after an `@` the relays where others can find you. For now it is the key alone, with a note that only people on the same relay will find your feed: your relay is on your own computer. [Subscribe to someone's feed](terse:{guide-subscribe}) says how to share a relay. The reader's help page (?) shows the address too.

## What is kept where

| Folder | Holds |
|---|---|
| `~/.config/terse` | your key, the feeds you follow, your relays |
| `~/.local/share/terse` | what programs keep, the relay's store |
| `~/.cache/terse` | articles and programs you downloaded |

On macOS the first is `~/Library/Application Support/terse`, the second the folder `data` in it and the third `~/Library/Caches/terse`. On Windows the first is `%APPDATA%\terse` and the others are both `%LOCALAPPDATA%\terse`. Wherever you are, setting `XDG_CONFIG_HOME`, `XDG_DATA_HOME` or `XDG_CACHE_HOME` to a folder puts the `terse` folder there instead.

## What you have now

A key, a name and an address: an identity no one issued and no one can take away. Next, put something in your feed: [Write and publish an article](terse:{guide-write}).

Profiles, names and addresses in full are in [The network](terse:{network}).
