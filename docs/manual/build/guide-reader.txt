# Make a reader

A Tersenet reader is any program that shows Tersenet articles to a person. An article is a plain text file that already reads well as it is; a reader shows it a little better, with headings that stand out and paragraphs wrapped to the window, and fetches articles and feeds from relays, checking that each is genuine. It needs no virtual machine: articles and the network are the whole of Tersenet's reading side.

This guide builds a small reader in three steps, each a complete program you can run:

- Step 1: show an article from a file, its headings and paragraphs.
- Step 2: fetch an article from a relay by its ID, and check its hash.
- Step 3: follow a feed: list its entries, check their signatures, open one.

The programs are in the Tersenet sources, in `examples/reader`, in Go and in Rust. Each step is a page or two of code, with a well-known library for each of WebSocket, BLAKE3 and Ed25519. Pick a language:

- [Make a reader in Go, step 1: show an article](terse:d34e710d10b181a81572acfa27b81545871a6ed5dc1b2491f2a1f3097f84058f)
- [Make a reader in Rust, step 1: show an article](terse:039b5a5543b4915da3c8e02b891b6f3821ab3e1c09413b078413bd7a93192b2f)

These programs are a start, not a whole reader: each page says what its program leaves out and where the complete readers do it.
