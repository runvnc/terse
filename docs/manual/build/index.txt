# The Terse manual

This manual describes the whole Terse system: the machine, its devices,
the network that carries articles and programs, and the programs that
come with it. It is written as Terse articles, so you can read it in the
reader, follow its links with Tab and Enter, and publish it to a feed.
Each article below says what it covers.

## Start here

- [What Terse is](terse:e046f9d71fd63fe686348ffcb114001691e411623ff19b006b25f3c39d3d09ef): what Terse is for, in one
  paragraph.
- [How Terse works](terse:085a00336e4ca45bcd19eece1e4189adfd3e15a834ddf81b67b2c2d0c772e748): the technical details in
  three pages: sharing articles and programs, the network, the machine
  and its frame budget, the devices, determinism and the shell.
- [Writing a program](terse:f26ef1ae2d5daccce214a09d468d77eaa672b08e615cc30066c735a0e2bf6d6b): the toolchain, the C
  library, building, running a program headless and in the host, and
  publishing it in a feed.
- [Using the shell and the reader](terse:1823a1dcb512982320f091420d51298ab8f80420790e2da67b08c6c3124ff0bd): the keys of the
  shell and the reader, layouts, sessions and the consent prompts.
- [Writing an article](terse:7a1608758c30131d63598142f26d342b98cbf1c3046b68e7b0be9d8cc93c5f55): the article format,
  every construct shown as source and as it is shown.
- [State, replay and conformance](terse:d7f67059be6fa22c61a51d29c13db78bdd913ee1c710d7fbcc3b3e5df5db6ecd): what a machine's state
  is, its hash, snapshots and the conformance image.

- The system device: frames, events, time, random bytes, controllers,
  exit, and asking the shell to open something.
- The terminal: an 80 by 30 grid of character cells.
- The 2D vector display: lines and filled shapes from a display list,
  in a clean or a phosphor style.
- Content: blobs fetched and published by hash, the feeds the host
  follows, search, and named records kept on the host.
- Sound: a 16-voice wavetable synthesizer, with pairing, feedback,
  ring modulation, noise and samples; optional, and bit-exact.
- The page: typeset proportional text, figures and tables in a
  rectangle of the screen.
- Machines: the shell's device, which starts other machines and lays
  out their screens, flat or as panels in its 3D scene.
- The 3D vector display: lines, points and circles in a box behind and
  in front of the screen, seen from an eye the host chooses.
